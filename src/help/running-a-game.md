# Running a game

For the GM: setting up the table, inviting players, and moving through scenes and issues.

## Starting a game

Make a game from Games › New game. It starts with issue 1 and an empty scene using your default tracker (Settings › New games). Bring in villains and templates from your library, or quick-add minions and challenges.

[Open Games](#/games)

## Inviting players

Start hosting, then share the game's code or invite link. Players join with heroes from their own library, on their own devices. Your device holds the game: players' actions come to you and you send everyone the result. Anyone with the code can also watch on a TV or second screen.

## The GM screen

The GM screen sits beside the table. Running shows whatever you're working on: a villain, a group, a challenge or the environment. Under it, the Bring in tab brings things onto the table, Scenes plans what's coming, and Notes is yours alone.

## Hidden

Opposition can start hidden, so you can set it up before the players see it. Players don't see hidden things at all, and hidden things don't count for anything on the table until you reveal them. Settings › New games › New opposition › Starts hidden sets the default.

## Turn order

The turn order runs along the top of the table. Whoever is acting picks who goes next from the Hand off to… menu. When everyone has acted, a new round starts. The table's ⋯ menu can fix mistakes: mark someone as acted or not, or set the round number.

## Scene tracker

Each scene has a tracker of boxes in green, yellow and red. Advance it as the scene goes on; the scene's zone is the color the tracker has reached. Settings › New games picks the tracker new games start with.

## Status die

A villain's status die is set by its archetype. Where it depends on the table, the app counts what it can see in the current scene and shows the count it used. Hidden things never count. Where the archetype needs your judgment, you enter the count or pick the condition, and you can always override it.

## Make the scene more dangerous

Once per scene, a villain can roll an Overcome to push the scene tracker forward. The Running panel opens the roll and asks if it succeeded; on a success the tracker advances one box.

## Villain twists

A villain's twists are listed on its Running panel. Add a minor twist there, and remove it when it's dealt with. A major twist ends the scene, so start the next scene instead.

## Challenges and Overcome

A challenge's card lists its steps. Players can roll Overcome against it from their own table, and the result comes to you to mark a step done. A challenge belongs to the GM or to the environment; only the environment's count toward a Domain villain's status die. Change it from the card's ⋯ menu.

## Scenes

Plan scenes ahead in the GM screen's Scenes tab. Starting the next scene lets you choose what leaves the table, what comes in, and what resets.

## Set aside

When a scene ends, opposition you don't need right now can be set aside instead of removed. It's kept with the game, off the table, and can come back in a later scene.

## Issues

When the story wraps up, Finish the issue from the scene's ⋯ menu or the Scenes tab. Play pauses, each player is offered a back issue for their hero, and they can spend hero points before you start the next issue.

## The display view

Open display view puts the table on a second window, sized for a TV. It shows what players see, never hidden things.
