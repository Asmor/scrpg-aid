# Minion, Lieutenant, and Environment Creation

Paraphrased processes for the GM's smaller building blocks. The options are in `data/minions/minions.json` and `data/environments/environments.json`. Page numbers are printed page numbers in the Core Rules.

## Minions (p. 204–206)

1. Name the minion and settle its concept.
2. Pick a die size (d4 weakest to d12 strongest). There's a table of example creatures per size. For a one-off scene, the scene difficulty chart (p. 186) suggests sizes.
3. Write a short description of what it is and how it threatens heroes.
4. Optionally add one or two abilities. Bonuses and penalties usually run +1 to +3, most often 2.
5. Optionally note its tactics.

Minions roll only their single die for any action. Large groups taking the same action roll together. Some minions are limited to one basic action. Minions and lieutenants never take major twists; they just fail (p. 156).

## Lieutenants (p. 207)

Built like minions, with these differences:

- A distinctive name, often a full name and a title.
- Usually d8–d12. A d6 lieutenant suits something weak but hard to get rid of.
- One or more abilities, which can be stronger than a minion's.
- They're hardier: a successful save doesn't lower their die.
- For truly enormous foes, use an environment plus challenges plus threats instead.

## Scene difficulty (p. 186)

With H = number of heroes (½H rounds up):

| Difficulty | Minions | Lieutenants | Villains | Environment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Easy | H × d6 | ½H × d8 | none | none |
| Moderate | H × d8 | ½H × d10 | minor villain | standard |
| Difficult | H × d10 | ½H × d12 | major villain | hostile |

Two minions can be traded for one lieutenant one die size larger.

## Environments (p. 240–245)

1. Name it. The name also sets its scale.
2. Give it three broad traits.
3. Give each trait a die (d4 minimal to d12 catastrophic; d8 is a fine default, d10 for the most important trait). The three dice form its pool.
4. For each zone (Green, Yellow, Red), write 2–3 minor twists and 1 major twist that get worse as the scene goes on. Twists use the environment's Min/Mid/Max dice to take basic actions, add threats, create challenges, or advance the scene tracker. Follow the strength guidelines for each zone and twist size.
5. Define any threats (minions/lieutenants) and challenges the twists bring in.

On the scene tracker's turn, the environment: advances the tracker; has its active threats act; then either introduces a new threat (if none are in play) or triggers a twist for the current zone.

## Challenges (p. 189–199)

- A challenge is a short description plus checkboxes. Each box is cleared by a successful Overcome.
- Aim for about 3 successes and no more than 5.
- Optional extras: a timer, alternative approaches, linked or branching follow-up challenges, an outcome when resolved, and a consequence when triggered.
- **Doomsday devices** are challenges with catastrophic stakes that take their own turns and advance the scene tracker.
