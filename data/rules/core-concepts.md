# Core Concepts

A short, paraphrased reference to the game terms that show up on character sheets and in ability text. It summarizes how the pieces work and is not a substitute for the rulebook. Page numbers are printed page numbers in the Core Rules.

## Dice (p. 17, 20)

- The game uses d4, d6, d8, d10, and d12. A d10 face showing 0 counts as 10.
- Powers and qualities are rated d6 (low) to d12 (high). A character without a fitting power or quality can use d4 in its place.
- Hero status dice are also rated d6 to d12. Some villain archetypes use status dice as low as d4.

## Dice pool and effect die (p. 20–21)

- A normal action rolls exactly three dice: one power, one quality, and one status die.
- After rolling, the three results are sorted into **Min**, **Mid**, and **Max** by the numbers rolled, not by die size. Ties can be ordered however the player likes.
- The **effect die** sets the result. It is the Mid die unless an ability or effect says otherwise ("use your Max die", "Max+Min dice", and so on).
- "Rolling your single [X] die" means rolling just that one die. Reactions usually work this way.

## GYRO: status zones (p. 14, 16)

- Characters and scenes are always in one of four zones: **G**reen, **Y**ellow, **R**ed, or **O**ut.
- A hero's zone comes from current Health compared with the Health ranges on the sheet. The scene's zone comes from the scene tracker. Use whichever is further along.
- The zone sets the status die and which abilities are available: Green abilities always; Yellow abilities in Yellow or Red; Red abilities only in Red. In the Out zone, only the Out ability is available.
- For a minor twist, a hero can use one ability from the next more severe zone (p. 19).

## Ability types (p. 12, 45)

- **A (Action):** used as the hero's action for the turn. Only one per turn.
- **R (Reaction):** triggered by a specific event. Each character gets one reaction per round, which resets at the start of their turn (p. 29).
- **I (Inherent):** always active.
- Text in [brackets] (such as [power], [quality], [element/energy], or [Boost or Hinder]) is a choice made once, during creation, and then written on the sheet. It changes only through advancement (p. 44, 142).

## Basic actions (p. 19, 24–29)

- **Attack:** deals damage equal to the effect die. Attacks always hit. Defenses and damage reduction lower the damage.
- **Defend:** the effect die reduces the next damage taken (by you or the target you protect) before your next turn. Multiple Defends don't stack.
- **Boost:** creates a bonus (positive mod).
- **Hinder:** creates a penalty (negative mod).
- **Overcome:** gets past an obstacle. The effect die is compared to the outcome table below.
- **Recover:** regains Health. Only available through abilities, or through montage scenes.

### Overcome outcomes (p. 25)

| Effect die | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| 0 or less | Spectacular failure |
| 1–3 | Failure, or success with a major twist |
| 4–7 | Success with a minor twist |
| 8–11 | Full success |
| 12+ | Success with an extra benefit |

### Mod sizes from Boost/Hinder (p. 26)

| Effect die | Mod |
| --- | --- |
| 0 or less | none |
| 1–3 | ±1 |
| 4–7 | ±2 |
| 8–11 | ±3 |
| 12+ | ±4 |

### Mod rules (p. 22, 26–27)

- A mod changes one effect die. Declare it before rolling.
- Mods are normally used up after one use.
- **Persistent** mods last until removed, until they stop making sense, or until the scene ends.
- **Exclusive** mods: a roll can use at most one exclusive bonus and one exclusive penalty.
- A mod can be removed with an Overcome, or by creating an opposing mod.

## Twists (p. 29–30)

- A twist is a complication the player accepts in exchange for success, or that comes from the environment.
- **Minor** twists cause small setbacks that clear at the next montage scene.
- **Major** twists cause serious setbacks whose mechanical effects last until the end of the issue.
- The twist questions from a hero's principles are prompts for choosing twists.

## Hero points and collections (p. 31–32)

- When a hero uses a principle in an Overcome action, every hero on the team gains a hero point. Meaningful social scenes also award hero points. Each hero can earn at most 5 per issue.
- At the end of an issue, hero points become exclusive bonuses (+1 to +4) whose sizes add up to the points spent. These are the "Hero Point Rewards" on the sheet. Unused points and bonuses don't carry over.
- After six back issues, the issues are combined into a named **collection**. Each collection can be used once per session to set one die to any value after a roll, to establish a fact from a past issue, or in place of taking a minor twist.

## Opposition basics (p. 16–17)

- **Villains** have full character sheets, like heroes.
- **Lieutenants** are a single die. When damaged, they roll that die as a save. If the save is lower than the damage, the die drops one size, and a lieutenant whose die would drop below d4 is defeated. Damage of at least double the die's size defeats a lieutenant outright.
- **Minions** are a single die. A failed save defeats a minion; a successful save drops its die one size. A minion already at d4 that succeeds on its save stays at d4. Groups of minions taking the same action roll together.

## Montage scenes (p. 32–33)

- A montage clears all minor twists and leftover mods (including persistent ones).
- Each hero picks one: recover Health to the top of the next zone up (with extra recovery available by taking twists), help another hero recover an extra zone, or prepare (a Boost for the next scene).
